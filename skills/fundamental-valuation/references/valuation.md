# Valuation method

## Separate the dates and denominators

Show the current quote timestamp, latest reporting period, forecast vintage and target valuation date. FY1 may be partly elapsed or recently ended but not yet reported; it is the first annual period after the latest reported full-year base. Compute return annualization from the quote date to the target date, not automatically three years. Do not mix a forward twelve-month multiple with FY3 earnings without identifying the different horizon.

Revenue and common net income should use a consistent currency, unit scale and accounting basis. EPS-derived common income requires a compatible forecast weighted-average diluted share count; market capitalization requires current economic shares outstanding. Future price requires a future diluted **valuation** share count, which can differ from weighted-average EPS shares. Resolve multiple classes and ADR ratios before comparing prices and earnings; convert all quantities to an equivalent common-share or ADR basis and disclose it.

## Build the actuals and forecasts

For FY0 and TTM inspect revenue, organic/constant-currency growth, gross and operating margins, reported common net income, normalized adjustments, diluted EPS, EBITDA, CFO, capex and FCF. Include cash, debt/maturities, preferred equity, minority interest, pension/lease treatment, SBC, issuance/buybacks and commitments where material. Define FCF (usually CFO minus capex) and its limitations; SBC may be added back in CFO but can still impose per-share dilution.

Build FY1–FY3 metric cells with value/range, fiscal end, estimate publication date, source, provider, accounting basis and estimate type. Use reported actuals, company guidance, dated consensus, individual analyst estimate, derived estimate or model scenario labels. Never turn a guidance midpoint or your growth extrapolation into analyst consensus. Two sites can redistribute the same vendor. Search missing years through IR, presentations and alternate public providers, then report any gap. A fourth “future” period is not needed merely to hide a missing first year.

For each annual step, growth = current-year metric / prior-year metric − 1 when the denominator is positive. The profitable strict screen needs revenue and common income to clear the configured threshold for all three steps on compatible bases. A negative/zero income base is a separate turnaround analysis; a later positive endpoint does not repair it. Report three-year CAGR only as a supplementary measure. A company with annual revenue growth of 30%, 2%, 30% can have strong CAGR and still fail.

Where useful, compare historical revenue, common-income, EPS and share-count growth with the forward path. This distinguishes operating growth from per-share growth and flags a forecast requiring a sharp, unexplained acceleration. For an earnings-gap candidate, match the last unaffected close, first full post-release session and benchmark window; examine changes versus prior guidance and dated pre-release consensus, rather than calling YoY growth an earnings beat.

When book equity or book value per share is requested, use an explicit bridge: opening common equity + common net income − common dividends − buybacks + net common-equity issuance + applicable OCI/other equity movements = closing common equity. Reconcile any other movements and divide by a compatible period-end share count. Do not forecast book value by merely retaining all net income when distributions, issuance or other equity changes are material. Book equity, market capitalization and estimated fair equity value are distinct.

## Select and justify the multiple

1. Choose an economically comparable peer set: sector alone is insufficient. Compare mix, geography, margins, growth, ROIC, leverage, capital intensity, recurring revenue and accounting basis. Disclose excluded outliers and the price/forecast dates used.
2. Compare peer median/range and the issuer's own historical valuation, with periods chosen to avoid anchoring on a bubble, distress year or peak cyclical earnings. Consider rates and the durability of excess returns.
3. Explain the proposed discount/premium in terms of cash conversion, balance sheet, competitive advantage and execution risk. Use a range. Do not mechanically convert a growth percentage into a P/E, or call a growth stock cheap because a screener PEG is below one.
4. Check terminal maturity: rapid near-term growth should not automatically earn today's growth multiple forever. Include a same-multiple/no-rerating case and slower-margin/growth cases.
5. Cross-check with cash-flow economics or a second suitable valuation basis. Different methods can disagree; show why rather than averaging incompatible measures.

## Equity, enterprise and per-share values

For P/E: future common equity = common net income × fair P/E. Dividing by current market cap gives a **ratio**; subtract one for aggregate equity-value change. Divide future equity by future diluted valuation shares to obtain future price. The price return may be much lower than aggregate growth when equity is issued.

For EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, EV/sales or EV/FCF, the earnings/cash-flow denominator must belong to the enterprise. Bridge enterprise value to common equity by subtracting debt, preferred claims and noncontrolling interests, and adding eligible cash/nonoperating assets, consistently treating leases and restricted cash. Avoid double-counting proceeds already in the cash balance. Future multiple scenarios use a consistent future balance sheet. Do not multiply revenue by EV/sales and label the result market cap without the bridge.

Future value is not current fair value. A present-value model must use a justified required return and appropriate cash-flow ownership: discount equity cash flows at cost of equity or enterprise cash flows at WACC, not one using the other's rate. If discounting terminal equity alone, state whether intervening distributions are omitted and whether retained cash is already in terminal value. Show nominal, currency-specific assumptions and sensitivity; do not imply a precise discount rate is known.

## Sector and special cases

| Business | Useful primary lenses | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profitable nonfinancial | Normalized P/E, EV/EBIT, cash-flow valuation | One-off tax profits or cyclically peak earnings as recurring |
| Banks / insurers | P/TBV or P/B with ROE, capital adequacy, credit/reserve quality; P/E | Treating deposits like ordinary industrial net debt |
| REITs / property | FFO/AFFO, NAV/cap rates, leverage and recurring capex | P/E-only ranking against software companies |
| Cyclicals / commodities | Mid-cycle earnings/cash flow, asset/cost curve and balance sheet | A low P/E at peak commodity earnings as automatic cheapness |
| Loss-making scale-up | EV/sales or gross profit linked to achievable mature margins; dilution/runway | Negative P/E; unsupported profit CAGR |
| Pre-revenue / binary asset | Milestone/probability scenarios and failure value | Treating TAM or one optimistic forecast as certain earnings |

## Scenarios and result

Vary operating drivers and funding/share counts as well as multiples. Show bear/base/bull, a no-rerating case, upside/downside and annualized per-share price return, excluded dividends/costs, thesis-breaking assumptions and what today’s price implies. Do not assign statistical probabilities without a defensible method. A fair multiple is a judgment supported by evidence, not a fact supplied by SEC filings.
